Population and sample

Goat Census

✦ · ✦

A village keeps 1,000 goats in four pastures. Each goat has a weight. The village wants to know the mean weight of all 1,000, but weighing every goat is slow, so a census taker weighs a sample. Draw the sample, describe the goats you weighed, then see what repeating the draw teaches.

The population is all 1,000 goats. A sample is the goats weighed. The data are their weights. A variable is a characteristic measured on each goat, here weight. A statistic is a number computed from the sample, such as its mean. A parameter is the number for the whole population, which the statistic estimates.

Station III · Describe
Describe the sample

Mode, median and mean are measures of centre. Range, quartiles, mean absolute deviation, variance and standard deviation are measures of spread.

Station IV · Repeat
Draw it a thousand times

Keep the strategy and size chosen above. Each draw gives one sample mean. The histogram collects them.

Law of large numbers

One simple random sample grows from 1 goat to 1,000. The running mean settles on the parameter.

Central limit theorem

The population of weights is skewed. The sample means are not: across draws they pile up in a bell around the parameter, and the bell narrows as the sample grows, by one over the square root of n.

iid: each goat drawn independently from the same population. Pens and friendship break independence, and the bell changes shape.

Station V · Infer
From the sample to the village

One sample gives one interval: the mean plus or minus 1.96 standard errors. Draw 100 samples and see how many intervals hold the parameter.

A probability sample is wrong by chance, and chance shrinks with size. A convenience sample is wrong by design, and size does nothing. The sampling rule decides what the data mean, just as the host does behind the three doors.
